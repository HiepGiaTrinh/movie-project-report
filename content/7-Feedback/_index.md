A few honest thoughts on my time with First Cloud AI Journey so far.

### Overall

**Working environment.** The Bitexco office is a decent place to work — professional but not stiff. People are around to help when something's not working.

**Support from the FCAJ team.** Honestly the thing I've gotten the most out of is the live architecture-review sessions run on the FCAJ group chat — the organizing team going through dozens of diagrams in one evening, calling out specifics (icon conventions, missing HA, weird service choices). It felt more like a real client review than a class, which pushed me to actually look things up instead of guessing.

**Relevance to my major.** The SageMaker work lines up well with my Computer Science background, and it's given me hands-on exposure to ML infrastructure that university just doesn't cover.

**Learning & growth.** Beyond the AWS services themselves, I picked up some MLOps habits — versioning models properly, evaluating with real metrics instead of eyeballing it, thinking about cold-start upfront. Also got better at explaining architecture to people who aren't deep into the technical side.

**Team culture.** Working with people who each own a different piece of the same system made things feel a lot more real than a typical school project — when something broke in my part, it actually affected everyone else's work too.

**Program logistics.** Being flexible about when to come in versus work remotely made it manageable to juggle this alongside classes.

### A few more thoughts

- **Best moment:** getting the recommendation endpoint to actually return sensible results end-to-end, after a few frustrating days stuck on preprocessing.
- **Could be better:** clearer guidance on the report/workshop format earlier — a lot of us, myself included, only found the detailed rubric partway through.
- **Would I recommend it:** yeah — the direct feedback culture on the FCAJ group is genuinely useful if you're willing to act on it.

### Suggestions

- Share the report rubric and example diagrams at the very start, not halfway through.
- A short session on AWS diagramming conventions early on would save a lot of people from getting the same feedback over and over.
- Would be interested in continuing to build on this project down the line.
